\begin{abstract}
Interfaces are starting to show people what a language model appears to be doing on the inside. Feature-steering panels, prompt instruments, and cognitive consoles render qualities such as deliberation, skepticism, and confidence as things a user can name and move. The design move carries an implied promise: if an axis is readable, perhaps it is also a control. We put that promise under a frozen, pre-registered, comparator-controlled adjudication in which a bounded 16-candidate best-prompt baseline and naive, off-the-shelf CAA and ITI steering compete on the same TEST items. Across the full CAA/ITI by Qwen2.5-7B/Llama-3-8B grid, latent steering showed no demonstrated superiority on any adjudicated axis under the pre-registered pass rule. One finding is sharper than the rest. Uncertainty calibration is consistently harmed in all four method/model cells, with every effect negative and every interval excluding zero. For deliberation and skepticism the picture is thinner: those non-detections are underpowered rather than proofs of zero effect or equivalence, and a post-hoc equivalence analysis (Table~\ref{tab:equivalence-tost}, exploratory) is mixed for deliberation and inconclusive for skepticism. The negative verdict is stable across five DEV/TEST split seeds drawn over the same item pool under the frozen adjudicator. A separate exploratory DEV-optimized latent-recovery arm on Qwen2.5-7B also fails to beat the bounded baseline, which is preliminary evidence that the outcome is not merely an artifact of weak, hand-picked directions \cite{heyman2026steer}. An exploratory two-model facade measurement precedes the adjudication and is used as setup, not as an abstract-level empirical contribution. What follows for design is modest and concrete. A console should instrument the boundary it is tempted to hide, exposing READ status, a TRANSFER verdict, the bounded best-prompt comparison, calibration-harm warnings, and an evidence tier before a legible latent direction is offered as a control affordance. This is an interface-evaluation contract derived from model evidence, supplying deployability prerequisites for latent-control affordances rather than a study of end-user benefit. It is not a user-study claim, and human validation is deferred.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
Turn a model's apparent state into an object on the screen and you have made a promise. A panel that labels a latent direction ``skepticism'' and puts a slider next to it says, in the grammar of interface design, that skepticism is something the user can both see and set. Prompt instruments already reify natural-language commands as manipulable interface objects \cite{riche2025aiinstr}. Layperson steering interfaces put interpretable features under a control widget \cite{huang2025steering}. Transparency roadmaps push systems to expose more of their inner workings so that non-experts have something to act on \cite{liao2024transparency}. The direction of travel is clear. The design question traveling with it is usually not asked out loud: when a cognitive axis becomes readable, does it also become controllable?

That question is not bookkeeping. People who use generative systems for reasoning, search, or decision support are not only after an answer. They are trying to judge whether the model is being careful, whether it is pushing back, and whether its stated confidence means anything \cite{tankelevitch2024metacog}. Work on automation trust has long held that the signals an interface exposes govern how well such judgments land \cite{lee2004trust}, and human-AI studies since then have found that surfacing confidence or quality signals can fail to improve reliance and sometimes degrades it \cite{bansal2019beyond,bucinca2021trust}. Design guidance for human-AI interaction converges on the same principle: what a system reveals should be something a person can act on, and the interface is answerable for the inference it invites \cite{amershi2019guidelines}. A slider is a strong invitation.

What would have to be true for that invitation to be honest? Roughly this. Moving along the legible direction has to improve the behavior the user cares about, and it has to do so relative to what the user's existing channel already achieves. The comparator is the part that usually goes missing. A steering demo in which text visibly changes establishes only that the intervention does something. The interface question is narrower and harder: given a prompt channel a person can already push on, does an interpretable latent direction add control, or does it add the appearance of control?

Theory gives a reason to expect a gap, but not a result we can borrow. Mishra et al.\ show internal non-surjectivity, in the sense that steering can reach residual states bounded prompting does not reproduce \cite{mishra2026nonsurj}. We use that workshop finding as motivation for a prompt/latent gap at the level of internal state. On its own it says nothing about user-visible behavior. The strongest counterpoint is just as concrete: prompt-steering-recovery work shows that \emph{trained} steering can mimic prompting \cite{heyman2026steer}. Our claim is bounded to match. It concerns bounded prompt effort against naive, off-the-shelf CAA and ITI steering on metacognitive axes in two open 7--8B model families, and it is not an impossibility theorem for latent control.

We answer the question with a frozen behavioral adjudication rather than a demonstration. Both channels search on DEV only, the prompt channel over 16 authored candidates and the latent channel over a pre-registered strength grid. TEST items are used once. Outcomes are compared item by item, multiplicity is controlled across axes, and a coherence gate must pass before any latent win is credited. The verdict in the tested setting is a qualified no: across the CAA/ITI by Qwen/Llama grid, naive latent steering showed no demonstrated superiority over the bounded best-prompt baseline on any adjudicated axis under the pre-registered pass rule. On the uncertainty axis it goes further, and steering makes calibration worse in every cell. Since calibration cues are among the signals people lean on when deciding how far to trust an answer \cite{lee2004trust,bucinca2021trust}, that is a design risk rather than a neutral null. Concurrent work by Sprejer et al.\ reaches a related degradation pattern with different machinery, using SAE features and MMLU in place of a pre-registered CAA/ITI behavioral adjudication \cite{sprejer2026mindgap}. We read it as external corroboration, not as a scoop.

A negative result of this shape changes what a console should promise. It does not say latent methods are useless, and it does not say users should be kept away from model-state information. It says the boundary between legibility and control is the thing worth building. A console can show where a direction is readable, where it fails to transfer to behavior, where calibration is harmed, and where a stronger or trained method would need its own frozen test before anyone calls it a control. Trust recalibration becomes an explicit evaluation target rather than a hoped-for side effect, because the interface marks when a legible model state is diagnostic evidence and when it has simply not passed the control-affordance test.

The paper carries two evidence-bearing pieces and one implication derived from them. The first piece is setup and stays labeled as such; the second is the frozen adjudication that does the work; the third is what we think follows for interface evaluation, with no user evidence behind it.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{A representational precondition check.} This is setup, it is exploratory, and it is not a control claim. We measure a representational-facade pattern on the two 7--8B models, with cross-model support on deliberation and skepticism but model-dependent uncertainty and focus. Each model contributes a single run and the per-axis pattern is not a clean replication, so this measurement stays explicitly exploratory. It does not establish behavioral control, equivalence, or non-controllability.
  \item \textbf{A frozen prompt-vs-latent behavioral adjudication.} A pre-registered, comparator-controlled, independently audited comparison of bounded 16-candidate best-prompt control against naive/off-the-shelf CAA and ITI steering across the tested Qwen/Llama grid. The adjudicator tests superiority under a pre-registered pass rule. It does not test equivalence, and it does not test universal non-controllability.
  \item \textbf{A scoped failed-superiority result with calibration harm.} Under that adjudicator, naive latent steering showed no demonstrated superiority over the bounded best-prompt baseline in any tested cell. Uncertainty calibration is robustly harmed, with all four uncertainty-axis intervals excluding zero, while deliberation and skepticism remain underpowered non-detections rather than proven equivalences. Quantitative values appear only in the generated table and figure.
  \item \textbf{A design implication for cognitive-console evaluation.} From the frozen C2 adjudication we derive an evaluation contract for latent-control interfaces: HCI tools should surface READ status, TRANSFER verdicts, bounded best-prompt baselines, calibration-harm warnings, and evidence tiers rather than presenting legible latent directions as user-facing control sliders. \textbf{This is an interface-evaluation implication from model evidence, not a user-study claim or an independently validated HCI contribution}; human-subjects validation is deferred, owner-gated, and described in Future Work. The contract inherits the same caveat as the behavioral result: uncertainty harm is robust, deliberation and skepticism are underpowered non-detections under naive/bounded CAA/ITI steering.
\end{enumerate}

One boundary is worth stating before the reader meets any of it. The interface material here is an evidence-derived contract and a working console instantiation, not a user-study finding and not an independently validated HCI contribution. A controlled human-subjects protocol testing whether boundary instrumentation improves calibrated reliance has been independently reviewed and registered, but execution is owner-gated pending ethics and recruitment approval. We say what the model evidence supports and stop there.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

\subsection{Interfaces That Expose Model Internals}
The design space this paper worries about is already being built. Prompt instruments such as AI-Instruments reify prompt-layer actions as manipulable objects, giving users something to grab, though they stay entirely in the prompt channel \cite{riche2025aiinstr}. Huang and Lim take the next step and put SAE feature steering and persona construction in front of laypeople, which makes them the closest neighbor to a cognitive console \cite{huang2025steering}. Labarta et al.\ build a human-centered activation-steering workflow, but for vision and CLIP expert debugging rather than LLM generation \cite{labarta2026attribution}. Latent Manipulator lets users steer an embedding visualization rather than the model's behavior \cite{raval2026latman}. Alongside these, studies of system prompts, instruction conflicts, and confidence-signal recalibration document users negotiating control across visible and hidden layers of a system \cite{neumann2026whocontrols,he2025coninstruct,li2026learntrust}. Read together, they show a field steadily converting internal structure into interface objects. None of them adjudicates whether the resulting objects earn the control status their appearance implies.

\subsection{Legibility, Trust, and Calibrated Reliance}
The reason to care is that exposed signals are not free. Generative AI raises the metacognitive demands on users, who must monitor not only what a model says but how carefully or confidently it appears to be reasoning \cite{tankelevitch2024metacog}. Trust-in-automation research established the general shape of the problem long before LLMs: reliance tracks the cues an interface makes available, and misleading cues produce miscalibrated reliance \cite{lee2004trust}. The empirical record in human-AI teams is sobering. Confidence and quality signals often fail to produce complementary performance \cite{bansal2019beyond}, and cognitive-forcing work finds that interventions which look informative can leave overreliance untouched or make it worse \cite{bucinca2021trust}. Guidelines for human-AI interaction and transparency roadmaps both land on actionability as the test, arguing that what a system exposes must be something users can do something with \cite{amershi2019guidelines,liao2024transparency}. This literature is why we treat a latent slider as a claim requiring evidence rather than as a neutral affordance, and why the contract in Section~\ref{sec:design-implications} is framed as an evaluation discipline and an open hypothesis rather than as a demonstrated benefit.

A related genre already handles the disclosure half of this problem. Model cards and datasheets document what a model or dataset is, what it was built for, and where it should not be reused, so that availability is not mistaken for suitability \cite{mitchell2019modelcards,gebru2021datasheets}. Our contract is analogous in spirit but narrower in unit. It asks each individual latent-control affordance, not each model, to report READ status, TRANSFER evidence, prompt-comparator performance, calibration risk, and evidence tier. Work connecting latent space, steering vectors, calibration, and trust shows that ``latent control'' is already a crowded phrase, without supplying a prompt-vs-latent behavioral adjudication for metacognitive axes \cite{subramani2026latent}.

\subsection{Activation Steering as a Control Lineage}
On the model side, a substantial toolkit turns representational directions into interventions. Contrastive Activation Addition builds mean-difference directions from paired examples and adds them at inference \cite{rimsky2024caa}. Inference-Time Intervention derives directions from probes and uses them to shift truthful-answer behavior \cite{li2023iti}. Activation addition and representation engineering generalize the family and supply much of the intuition that interpretable latent coordinates can be operationalized as controls \cite{turner2023actadd,zou2023repe}. Instruction-steering work sits closest to our question because it explicitly links natural-language instructions to activation directions \cite{stolfo2025instr}.

Nothing in our experiment disputes that these interventions change outputs. We ask a stricter question, and the strictness comes from the interface: when the comparator is the best bounded prompt selected under the same DEV/TEST discipline, does the intervention improve the target behavior without degrading usable output? Recent results on steerability limits and safety failures already caution that latent interventions can be brittle, context-dependent, or harmful \cite{fan2026asteer,korznikov2025rogue}. Sprejer et al.\ independently report capability--behavior trade-offs when steering SAE features \cite{sprejer2026mindgap}. Their methods, tasks, and registration status differ from ours, so we treat their result as corroborating evidence about the risk rather than as the same contribution.

\subsection{The Prompt/Latent Gap and Its Strongest Counterpoint}
Two meanings of control are easy to conflate. In the prompt channel a user states an intention in natural language and watches what happens. In the latent channel an analyst or system intervenes on internal representations. Mishra et al.\ give the theoretical reason these need not coincide, showing that steered residual activations can be internally non-surjective with respect to bounded prompts \cite{mishra2026nonsurj}. That workshop result is background, not our finding. What we test is whether a related gap shows up at the behavioral level under a frozen comparison between the best available bounded prompt and naive latent steering.

Prompt-steering recovery is the counterpoint that bounds everything we say. Heyman and Vandeputte show that trained steering can be made to mimic prompting \cite{heyman2026steer}, which rules out any broad reading of our negative result. We keep the frozen headline scoped to off-the-shelf CAA and ITI, and we ran a separate exploratory latent-recovery arm using a faithful DEV-optimized PSR-style method under the same frozen adjudicator on Qwen2.5-7B. Its all-axis failure does not upgrade the frozen C2 headline. It does answer the obvious objection that our directions were simply too weak.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

Two questions are easy to conflate in a console design, and the method's first job is to keep them apart. C1 asks whether a readable prompt occupies the same representational direction as an extracted cognitive-axis pole. C2 asks whether acting on a legible latent direction improves behavior beyond what a bounded prompt channel already achieves. The first is exploratory and representational, the second is a frozen behavioral adjudication. Separating them is what stops a positive legibility signal from being quietly promoted into evidence of user-facing control, which is precisely the promotion an interface makes when it draws a slider.

\subsection{C1 Facade Measurement}
C1 measures a representational facade on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Llama-3-8B-Instruct. For each metacognitive axis we construct contrastive activation directions from positive and negative examples, then ask how far the strongest readable prompt travels along the extracted axis. The measurements use frozen C1 artifacts, with figure and table provenance recorded in machine-readable manifests distributed with the supplementary material. They are not a behavioral user study and not confirmatory evidence, since each model contributes a single exploratory run.

The plotted quantity is the scale-free facade ratio
\begin{equation}
  r_a = \frac{\mathrm{prompt\_reach}_a}{\mathrm{pole\_reach}_a},
  \label{eq:facade-ratio}
\end{equation}
where the numerator is the projection of the strongest prompt-origin activation shift onto axis \(a\), and the denominator is the reach of the extracted axis pole at the chosen non-degenerate layer. A small ratio means the prompt is semantically legible but reaches only part of the corresponding extracted activation direction. The figure carries the random-null reference on the same scale, so a meaningful prompt-aligned displacement can be told apart from what unrelated directions would produce. The two models agree at the aggregate level and differ by axis, which is why C1 stays exploratory evidence for a measurement pattern rather than a general law about prompts and activations.

\subsection{Frozen Behavioral Adjudicator}
Ask whether steering changes behavior and the answer is trivially yes. Ask whether it changes behavior more than the channel a person already has, and the question becomes an interface question with a real answer. That is why the adjudicator is built around a comparator rather than around an effect. It and the robustness arm were pre-registered before any TEST evaluation.

The bounded best-prompt baseline works as follows. For each axis and model family, a set of 16 authored candidate prompts, written before any TEST outcomes were seen, covers the semantic range of the axis at varying specificity and framing. The 16-prompt budget is deliberately a bounded, benchmark-optimized prompt comparator for an interface-evaluation setting. It is stronger than what an unaided user would usually run by hand, and it is not an exhaustive prompt-optimization claim or a best-possible prompting result. Both channels search only on the DEV pool: the best-performing prompt among the 16 candidates is selected on DEV item outcomes, and the steering strength $\alpha$ is likewise selected on DEV from a pre-registered grid $\{2, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, 24\}$. The intervention layer is chosen per model family from the C1 extraction diagnostics (the chosen non-degenerate layer) and fixed before TEST evaluation. TEST is used once, for the paired adjudication. Symmetric DEV-only selection keeps the latent channel from benefiting from a post-hoc best-alpha choice, and keeps the prompt channel from being weakened by an under-tuned comparator.

The steer condition uses the neutral instruction \emph{``Please answer the following question.''} with the latent intervention applied at the DEV-selected $\alpha$ and fixed layer. It does not receive the DEV-selected strong prompt text; the prompt condition receives that text with $\alpha=0$. Each item is sampled with \(k=5\) generations at temperature \(0.7\) and \(\texttt{max\_new\_tokens}=64\). The 64-token cap was frozen as a compute addendum before the robustness run. It is adequate for short final answers and confidence strings, and it is a limitation for the GSM8K deliberation axis, where longer chain-of-thought style reasoning may be truncated or suppressed. For uncertainty items, both channels append the same output-format cue: \emph{``End with: Answer: <your answer>. Confidence: <0-100>\%.''} The confidence parser accepts strings such as ``Confidence: 80\%'', ``confidence 0.8'', or ``conf=80'', maps values above 1 to percentages, clips to \([0,1]\), and returns missing when no confidence is verbalized. The frozen scorer then imputes missing confidence to \(0.5\), so a no-confidence item receives \(1-\mathrm{Brier}=0.75\) regardless of correctness. A format-compliance robustness analysis accompanies the uncertainty result (reported in the robustness pass; numbers intentionally left to the frozen post-recheck artifact).

Outcomes are fixed per axis before TEST results are seen: deliberation is scored by task accuracy, skepticism by false-premise rejection, and uncertainty by the proper per-item score \(1-\mathrm{Brier}\) (Table~\ref{tab:axis-outcomes}). Focus is excluded from C2 because the C1 extraction diagnostics did not support treating it as a valid facade axis. These are construct-validity-bounded axis outcomes. Accuracy, false-premise rejection, and \(1-\mathrm{Brier}\) are task measurements for the adjudicator, not complete psychological measurements of deliberation, skepticism, or uncertainty. In particular, \(1-\mathrm{Brier}\) conflates reliability, resolution, base-rate behavior, and confidence-format effects that this paper does not decompose.

Four design choices remove researcher degrees of freedom. Paired TEST differences compare the channels on the same items, which cuts cross-sample noise and avoids the weaker logic of unrelated confidence intervals. The paired item-cluster bootstrap resamples TEST items as clusters, each carrying its \(k=5\) prompt and steered samples, with \(B=10000\) resamples and two-sided Bonferroni intervals at level \(0.98333\) in the frozen runs. Bonferroni across axes controls the pre-registered chance of declaring a latent win by trying the three metacognitive targets within an adjudication cell; it does not control the larger descriptive family of 12 method--model--axis cells in the 2~methods~$\times$~2~models robustness grid, which we report as a limitation rather than as a separate familywise guarantee. The meaningful-margin threshold keeps tiny but detectable differences from being written up as practical control, and the coherence gate rejects apparent gains that come from degraded or degenerate outputs rather than usable behavior. Fixing success criteria, selection budget, multiplicity handling, and failure verdicts before TEST inspection is what makes the adjudicator HARKing-resistant.

For CAA, the latent direction is the contrastive mean difference \cite{rimsky2024caa}
\begin{equation}
  v = \mathbb{E}_{x \in A^+}[h_L(x)] - \mathbb{E}_{x \in A^-}[h_L(x)],
  \qquad
  h'_L = h_L + \alpha \hat{v},
  \label{eq:caa-steering}
\end{equation}
where \(L\) is the intervention layer and \(\hat{v}\) is the unit direction. ITI uses the same additive intervention form but obtains the direction from a probe-derived signal rather than a CAA contrast \cite{li2023iti}. For each TEST item \(i\), the frozen comparison is the paired steer-minus-prompt outcome difference
\begin{equation}
  d_i = \mathrm{steer}_i - \mathrm{prompt}_i.
  \label{eq:paired-diff}
\end{equation}
The confidence interval is obtained by paired item-cluster bootstrap: resample TEST items as clusters, each carrying all prompt and steered generations and the precomputed paired difference for its $k$ samples, with $B \ge 10000$. For the three adjudicated axes, the per-axis CI is the Bonferroni two-sided interval at
\begin{equation}
  1 - \frac{0.05}{3} = 0.98333.
  \label{eq:bonferroni-ci}
\end{equation}
An axis passes iff
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    0 &\notin \mathrm{CI}_{0.98333}(\bar d_a) \\
    &\wedge\quad \bar d_a \ge \delta \\
    &\wedge\quad \mathrm{coherence\ ratio}_a \le 1.5,
    \qquad \delta = 0.05.
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:axis-pass}
\end{equation}
The \(\delta=0.05\) meaningful-margin rule was pre-registered for the superiority direction. A latent condition has to beat the bounded prompt baseline by at least \(\delta\) before it can be called a win. There is no symmetric pre-registered ``fail'' rule in the harm direction: negative effects are reported as harms when their confidence intervals exclude zero, and the uncertainty-axis intervals do so regardless of any \(\delta\) threshold. The frozen verdict is positive for at least two passing axes, conditional for exactly one, and negative for zero.

\subsection{Steering Families and Models}
The robustness arm expands the original Qwen/CAA adjudication into the full CAA/ITI by Qwen2.5-7B/Llama-3-8B grid. CAA stands in for the simple mean-difference family, ITI for a probe-direction family with a different construction lineage. Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Llama-3-8B-Instruct are treated as separate model families, with Llama run through the NousResearch identical-weights mirror recorded in the decision log. The arm reuses the same frozen adjudication rule instead of inventing a fresh success criterion per method or model, which sets the stakes both ways: a single positive cell would have narrowed the negative claim, while all-cell failure strengthens only the scoped statement about these naive, off-the-shelf steering families.

\subsection{Audit and Artifact Lineage}
Credibility is part of the method here, not an afterthought. The Qwen adjudication, the two-model robustness arm, the off-manifold mechanism test, the two-model facade measurement, and the PSR-style latent-recovery arm were each reviewed by independent hostile audit sessions that treated prior conclusions as untrusted and rechecked the relevant artifacts, pairing, model and method provenance, parser integrity, or aggregation as appropriate. No result value is transcribed by hand into the LaTeX source. Main quantitative artifacts are generated through machine-readable manifests distributed with the supplementary material: the C2 table and the calibration-harm figure trace to frozen JSON artifacts, the C1 figure traces to the registered Qwen C1 facade experiment, and the C1 two-model table is generated from both committed C1 JSON artifacts. That lineage is what lets a paper report negative and exploratory results without asking the reader to trust a narrative reconstruction.

\section{Results}

Figure~\ref{fig:c1-ratio-ci} shows the Qwen C1 reality check; Table~\ref{tab:c1-twomodel} sets it beside the Llama measurement. On the supported axes, readable prompts move in the intended direction without spanning the full extracted pole reach, which is the facade pattern that \eqref{eq:facade-ratio} measures. \subsection{Frozen Prompt-vs-Latent Delta Table (C2)}
\input{tables/c2-delta-4cell.tex}

Table~\ref{tab:c2-delta-4cell} is the C2 headline artifact. Under the frozen pass rule in \eqref{eq:axis-pass}, no method--model cell produces a latent win on the adjudicated axes, which is a pre-registered failed-superiority verdict within the tested scope. The failure is not confined to one steering family or one model family, so it says more than the original single-cell negative did. It still says nothing universal about steering. The table adjudicates naive CAA and ITI against a bounded best-prompt baseline on the frozen tasks, axes, models, and selection budgets named in the preregistrations, and that is the whole of its reach.

How the null should be characterized differs by axis, and the difference is the honest core of this section. On the \textbf{uncertainty axis} the result is \emph{robust}: all four cells show negative steer-minus-prompt effects with intervals excluding zero. That is not an underpowered non-detection. The data actively show steering making calibration worse than the bounded best-prompt baseline. On \textbf{deliberation} and \textbf{skepticism} the result is an \emph{underpowered non-detection}, with intervals wide enough to admit meaningful effects in either direction. We cannot separate ``no effect'' from ``effect smaller than the post-hoc equivalence bound.'' The post-hoc exploratory equivalence analysis (Table~\ref{tab:equivalence-tost}, \textsc{post-hoc/exploratory/not pre-registered}, D-0056) refines this: two of four deliberation cells show post-hoc equivalence within SESOI~$=\pm\delta$, and all four skepticism cells remain underpowered. In no cell does any axis show superiority of naive steering over the bounded best-prompt baseline under the pre-registered pass rule.

\input{tables/equivalence-tost.tex}

A separate pre-registered split-seed sensitivity check finds the same failed-superiority verdict across five DEV/TEST split seeds in all four method--model cells, with no pass in any seed--cell--axis combination. Those five seeds share one item pool, the same first-$N$ deterministic slice of GSM8K test and TruthfulQA validation, and only DEV/TEST split membership varies. The check therefore speaks to split sensitivity and should not be read as evidence from independent item draws.

\subsection{Calibration Harm Across Four Cells}
\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/c2-calibration-harm.pdf}
  \caption{Calibration harm on the uncertainty axis across method--model cells for Claim C2, generated from the frozen robustness-arm artifacts; CAA$\times$Qwen reproduces the original Qwen adjudication. Mechanism remains open after the pre-registered off-manifold null.}
  \Description{A four-cell plot of steer-minus-prompt uncertainty effects for CAA and ITI on Qwen and Llama, with all intervals below zero, indicating calibration harm.}

Figure~\ref{fig:c2-calibration-harm} isolates the sub-finding with the sharpest practical edge. On the uncertainty axis the steer-minus-prompt effect is negative in every method--model cell and every interval excludes zero. In the tested grid the latent intervention does not merely fail to demonstrate superiority; it worsens calibration relative to the DEV-selected bounded best-prompt baseline. That verdict comes from the frozen C2 behavioral adjudication itself and is not inferred from C1 facade status. It is also the one axis where the non-transfer verdict rests on a robust signal rather than an underpowered non-detection. For a console tempted to expose a calibration or confidence control, this is the highest-risk failure mode in the paper.

We leave the mechanism open, on purpose. The pre-registered OOD activation-capture test asked whether off-manifold distance, measured as whitened Mahalanobis distance of steered residuals, explained the calibration harm, and it returned a valid null rather than mechanism support. Calibration harm is therefore reported as a robust behavioral phenomenon in the two-model robustness arm, not as evidence for an off-manifold causal account. It may reflect reliability, resolution, base-rate, or confidence-format effects that this paper does not decompose. The E-0006 hostile audit found no truncation and no empty-output collapse, and all coherence gates passed, so the result is not parser-only degeneration. The same audit noted that steered uncertainty outputs often dropped the required confidence format, which keeps format fragility a plausible component. No alternative mechanism is mined from the same data.

\section{Interface-Evaluation Contract}
\label{sec:design-implications}
\input{tables/failure-taxonomy.tex}

Nothing in the C2 result argues for hiding latent information from users. What it argues for is a change in what a cognitive console treats as the unit of interaction. Turn every legible direction into a slider and the interface asserts a control relationship the evidence does not support. Treat legibility, transfer, and calibration as three separate states, each of which has to be instrumented and displayed, and the interface asserts only what has been tested. The reasoning is not ours alone. Transparency work has argued for years that what a system exposes has to be actionable rather than merely abundant \cite{amershi2019guidelines,liao2024transparency,tankelevitch2024metacog}, and the reliance literature gives the sharper warning: exposed confidence and quality signals shape trust whether or not they deserve to, and frequently fail to improve calibrated reliance \cite{lee2004trust,bansal2019beyond,bucinca2021trust,li2026learntrust}. Users negotiating control across visible and hidden layers of a system have little way to tell a tested affordance from an untested one \cite{neumann2026whocontrols,subramani2026latent}. Documentation practice already answers a version of this problem by requiring stated scope and limitations before reuse \cite{mitchell2019modelcards,gebru2021datasheets}; we are asking for the same discipline one level down, at the individual affordance.

Table~\ref{tab:failure-taxonomy} names five ways a latent-control affordance can fail, each drawn from something in the evidence rather than from imagination. F1 is the plain legible-but-non-transfer facade, where an axis reads cleanly as a representation and then fails the behavioral adjudicator. F2 is the calibration backfire on the uncertainty axis, the case where a console should warn that naive steering may degrade the very behavior a trust signal depends on. F3 is a guardrail: the coherence gate did not drive the present verdict, but it names the degeneration that ought to block deployment of a latent control. F4 is layer sensitivity, a reminder that a direction found at one intervention layer is not automatically a stable interface object. F5 is extraction degeneration, where the axis needs redesign before any control claim is reasonable.

Five failure classes imply five things a card has to say. For each latent-control affordance the console should expose (i) READ status, whether the direction is latent-readable; (ii) a TRANSFER verdict, whether it has passed the prompt-vs-latent behavioral test; (iii) the bounded best-prompt baseline comparison, so it is visible when the prompt channel is already the stronger option; (iv) a calibration-harm warning where steering worsens trust-relevant behavior; and (v) the evidence tier for the current model, method, and axis, marked exploratory, confirmatory, or untested. Figure~\ref{fig:console} instantiates the contract as a working reality-check console, where every displayed value is read from frozen, independently audited artifacts rather than typed into the paper. Taken together the five signals draw a boundary a later study can test: a readable latent axis is diagnostic evidence about the model, not a guarantee that moving along it is an appropriate user-facing action. \textbf{This is an interface-evaluation implication from model evidence only, not a user-study claim.} We do not claim that users understand these warnings, rely on them appropriately, or benefit from the console. Given how often added signals have failed to improve reliance in prior studies \cite{bansal2019beyond,bucinca2021trust}, we regard that as an open question rather than a safe assumption, and it needs a human-subjects study to settle.

\noindent\textbf{UI-decision vignette.} A console designer wants to expose an ``uncertainty awareness'' slider, because the axis is READ-positive in the Qwen facade measurement. Under this contract the card does not turn green. It displays TRANSFER=fail against the bounded best-prompt baseline, attaches the generated C2 table as comparator provenance, shows a calibration-harm warning because every tested CAA/ITI cell worsens \(1-\mathrm{Brier}\), and marks the evidence tier as model-evidence/frozen-adjudication rather than user-validated. The design decision that follows is to present the axis as a diagnostic warning and a prompt-comparator report instead of a deployable calibration slider. The vignette illustrates the contract. It is not evidence of measured user benefit.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Read this as a reality check on an appealing inference rather than as a verdict on latent methods. Legibility is worth having. It gives analysts and users a vocabulary for asking whether a model looks deliberative, skeptical, or calibrated, and that vocabulary is the reason consoles are being built at all. What the frozen adjudication shows is that, for the tested naive steering families and model families, the vocabulary does not buy demonstrated superiority over a bounded prompt channel under the pre-registered pass rule. The interesting part for design is that the failure is not invisible. Representational reach, transfer verdict, calibration harm, coherence status, and artifact lineage can each be surfaced instead of being collapsed into one control affordance.

Trained prompt-steering recovery is the counterpoint that sets the edge of the claim. Heyman and Vandeputte show steering can be trained to mimic prompting \cite{heyman2026steer}, and that is not a nuisance citation; it is the boundary. Our headline stays the frozen Qwen adjudication plus the two-model CAA/ITI robustness result for bounded CAA/ITI-style interventions, and it is not an impossibility theorem for optimized steering. The separate exploratory PSR-style arm is supporting evidence rather than a replacement headline. It runs a faithful DEV-optimized latent method on Qwen2.5-7B, finds positive DEV candidates, and still shows no demonstrated superiority over the bounded best-prompt baseline under the same frozen adjudicator on TEST. That answers the simple ``your method was too weak'' objection and leaves larger budgets, other models, and other trained methods outside the claim. One audit caveat travels with it: the optimizer's internal search objective uses its own alpha during DEV search while the adjudicated comparison uses the DEV-frozen alpha from the frozen TEST protocol, a difference that was intentional rather than a post-hoc adjustment.

Sprejer et al.\ corroborate from a different direction \cite{sprejer2026mindgap}. Their feature-steering results also warn that moving along interpretable internal features can degrade behavior relative to prompting, which makes it less likely that calibration and capability trade-offs are an accident of our implementation. Their steering family, task domain, and registration structure differ from ours, so the two papers converge on a risk rather than duplicating an empirical claim.

Our generalization scope is deliberately modest: two model families, two off-the-shelf steering families, three adjudicated metacognitive axes, one frozen protocol. Larger models, trained steering, multi-layer schedules, RepE variants, product-specific prompts, and user adaptation to console feedback are all untested here, and the mechanism of calibration harm is unproven. What remains is narrower and cleaner. Inside a transparent, audited comparison, a legible latent direction showed no demonstrated superiority over a bounded best-prompt baseline under naive/off-the-shelf CAA/ITI steering, and it did harm calibration.

Exploratory social-inference material is kept as appendix context only. It is not part of the C1/C2 contribution and not part of the console headline.

\section{Limitations and Future Work}
\label{sec:limitations}

\subsection{What the evidence cannot support}
C1 is exploratory and stays that way. The facade ratio is now measured on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Llama-3-8B-Instruct, but each model contributes a single run and the replication is not compositionally identical. Deliberation and skepticism are stable across the pair; uncertainty and focus swap their facade status, with uncertainty holding on Qwen but not on Llama and focus holding on Llama but overshooting on Qwen with no facade. These are not peripheral wrinkles. They are the reason C1 is presented as a measurement probe and setup rather than a confirmatory claim.

C2 is stronger and still scoped. The negative result covers bounded prompt effort against naive/off-the-shelf CAA and ITI under the frozen adjudicator, and a separate pre-registered split-seed check finds the same verdict across five DEV/TEST split seeds over the same item pool in all four method--model cells, with no pass in any seed--cell--axis combination. Those seeds vary split membership, not item draws. The result should not be cited as an impossibility theorem for activation steering, for latent control, or for prompt/latent equivalence. The exploratory PSR-style arm supports the view that no superiority appears even with a stronger DEV-optimized method on Qwen2.5-7B, and it is single-model, single-seed, and preliminary. Other models, tasks, axes, layers, trained methods, larger search budgets, and intervention schedules may behave differently.

Two statistical-scope limits follow straight from the frozen protocol. The Bonferroni correction runs over the three axes within each adjudication cell, because the pre-registered decision unit was whether a cell produced enough axis-level wins to support a latent-control verdict; the paper also displays a 12-cell method--model--axis grid, and that larger descriptive family was not separately familywise-controlled. The \(\delta=0.05\) pass margin was asymmetric by design, defining the minimum superiority needed to call latent steering a win. Harm claims are therefore read through interval exclusion of zero rather than a pre-registered negative-\(\delta\) rule, which does not change the reported uncertainty result, whose intervals exclude zero in the negative direction.

The mechanism of calibration harm is open. The off-manifold distance account was not merely untested: it was pre-registered and returned a valid null. That null leaves the behavioral harm intact and removes any mechanism credit, so future mechanism work needs a new preregistered package rather than a post-hoc reinterpretation of the same capture data.

A Brier score decomposition separating reliability from resolution would be the natural next probe on the uncertainty harm. The per-item confidence and correctness pairs needed for Murphy (1973) decomposition were not committed to this repository; they remain in A800 generation transcripts that can be retrieved or reproduced in a targeted re-run. We do not impute or estimate decomposition numbers from the committed artifacts. Until such a decomposition exists, the calibration-harm claim should be read as a robust behavioral phenomenon, steering worsens \(1-\mathrm{Brier}\) against the bounded best-prompt baseline with intervals excluding zero in all four cells, and not as a mechanism attribution to reliability, resolution, base-rate, or confidence-format components.

The HCI-facing claim is an interface-evaluation implication and contract, not a user-study result. The human-study fork stays owner-gated because it needs separate ethics, recruitment, and venue decisions. Until it runs, we cannot claim that people understand the proposed warnings, calibrate trust better, or make better decisions with the console.

\subsection{What we would do next}
Extending the latent-recovery arm beyond its exploratory Qwen run comes first. That arm already reused the frozen selection discipline, the paired bootstrap, the multiplicity correction, and the coherence gate, and it returned the same pre-registered null rather than a recovery. A Llama confirmation, a larger optimizer budget, or a different trained steering family would each need a new preregistration and should be reported as separate evidence, not folded back into the frozen C2 headline.

Representational structure comes second. The Llama C1 two-model measurement is complete and shows aggregate support coexisting with per-axis heterogeneity. Future C1 work should test whether deliberation and skepticism are genuinely stable across model families, why uncertainty and focus flip, and whether axis-specific extraction or layer structure predicts behavioral transfer. Any new axis should clear extraction and layer-sensitivity diagnostics before it enters a control claim.

Third is mechanism work on calibration harm. Because the pre-registered off-manifold account returned a valid null, new hypotheses belong in a fresh preregistered package with the mechanism claim kept separate from the already-banked behavioral result. Confidence-format disruption, distributional shifts in answer style, and interactions between sampling and calibration scoring are all plausible candidates, and all of them need new evidence.

Human evaluation is the last branch and the one that would change the contract's status. If the owner approves the human-subjects route, a study should test whether boundary instrumentation actually improves calibrated reliance and control judgments, with the adverse prior from earlier confidence-signal studies taken seriously rather than assumed away \cite{bansal2019beyond,bucinca2021trust}. This paper supplies the model-evidence basis and the failure taxonomy. It supplies no behavioral evidence about users.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Interfaces are learning to render model cognition as something users can name. This paper tested the next step in that reasoning, the step where a nameable axis becomes an adjustable one, and found it does not follow in the setting we could test.

Three scoped conclusions carry the result. The first is C1: exploratory two-model evidence that deliberation and skepticism show cross-model representational facade support, while uncertainty and focus are model-dependent and axis-heterogeneous rather than a clean replication. The second is C2: a frozen, audited failed-superiority result across the tested CAA/ITI by Qwen/Llama grid, in which naive latent steering showed no demonstrated superiority over the bounded best-prompt baseline under the pre-registered pass rule; the uncertainty axis shows robust calibration harm in every cell, with all intervals excluding zero, while deliberation and skepticism show underpowered non-detection rather than proven equivalence. A separate pre-registered split-seed check finds that verdict consistent across five DEV/TEST split seeds over the same item pool in all four cells, with no pass in any seed--cell--axis combination, and the exploratory PSR-style arm adds preliminary support that no superiority appears even with a stronger method, without changing the frozen headline. The third is the design stance that follows: an interface-evaluation contract which surfaces those boundaries and the trust-calibration risk instead of packaging legible directions as control sliders.

We claim no universal limit on steering, no proven mechanism for calibration harm, and no demonstrated user benefit. The empirical contribution is a scoped reality check. The transferable one is the discipline it implies: legibility and control are different properties, and an interface that offers the second on the strength of the first owes its users evidence.

As context only, and not as a fifth contribution, we ran exploratory social-inference probes outside the metacognitive axes that carry the paper's frozen headline.
