\begin{abstract}
Placing a slider beside a named latent property promises that moving the slider will control the named behavior. Output movement is too weak a criterion for that promise because users already have a prompt channel. We introduce a comparative evidence gate: a latent axis earns actionability only after a preregistered behavioral test shows improvement over a bounded prompt comparator while preserving coherence. A five-field contract records the gate's interface-facing evidence as READ, TRANSFER, comparator, calibration warning, and evidence tier, and a console artifact renders the resulting diagnostic, unresolved, or withheld-control state. We apply this procedure to bounded naive single-layer CAA/ITI steering across Qwen2.5-7B and Llama-3-8B, with DEV-only channel selection, paired TEST comparisons, positive controls, and frozen decision rules. The complete grid yields 0 of 12 axis-cell passes; 5 of 5 DEV/TEST split seeds preserve that verdict over the shared item pool. Endpoint and instrument checks register prompt and random-direction behavioral changes, while scale-corrected CAA still supplies no passing latent behavioral positive control within the coherent range. A readable axis should therefore become a control only after comparative behavioral evidence qualifies it, within the tested models, tasks, bounded prompt budget, and method-specific additive interventions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Alex is drafting a difficult message with an AI assistant and sees a slider labeled ``skepticism.'' Moving it upward appears to promise that the assistant will challenge a false premise more reliably. The widget makes a control claim. Yet the evidence commonly used to justify latent controls, such as readable features or changed outputs, does not show that the latent route improves behavior beyond instructions that a user can already give.

This paper changes the metric. A candidate latent affordance must demonstrate reliable incremental behavioral control over a bounded prompt channel, not merely movement in activations or text. The comparator is deliberately strong but fair. Sixteen authored prompt candidates cover varied wording and specificity; prompt and latent settings are selected on DEV only, and neither channel sees TEST during selection. The bounded channel is usable and reconstructable. It is harder to dismiss than a single casual prompt.

The problem matters at the interface. People monitor care, uncertainty, and resistance to faulty premises while using generative systems \cite{tankelevitch2024metacog}, and interface signals shape trust and reliance \cite{lee2004trust}. Confidence and explanation cues do not reliably improve calibration; some interventions intended to reduce over-reliance have weak or adverse effects \cite{bansal2019beyond,bucinca2021trust}. Human-AI guidance consequently links exposed information to an available action \cite{amershi2019guidelines,liao2024transparency}. A latent slider supplies that action visually, whether or not its behavioral qualification has been tested.

Prompt instruments, feature-steering interfaces, human-centered activation-steering workflows, and latent-space manipulation tools already bring model structure to the interaction surface \cite{riche2025aiinstr,huang2025steering,labarta2026attribution,raval2026latman}. Their visibility makes qualification urgent. Mishra et al.\ show that steering can reach residual states that bounded prompting does not reproduce \cite{mishra2026nonsurj}, but internal non-surjectivity does not entail behavioral advantage. Trained steering can also mimic prompting \cite{heyman2026steer}. The right question is therefore local and comparative: has this method, on this axis and model, earned actionability over the existing channel?

We ask:
\begin{quote}
\emph{What evidence should a latent-control interface require before a readable axis is presented as an actionable control?}
\end{quote}
We answer with a comparative evidence gate and its five-field record format. READ records whether a label has representational support. TRANSFER records the preregistered behavioral verdict; the bounded comparator states what the existing prompt channel achieved; the warning preserves contrast-specific measurement limits; and the evidence tier prevents a result from migrating across models or methods. The contract follows the observed failure modes rather than preceding them as a generic checklist.

The worked evaluation covers two 7--8B instruction-model families and two naive additive steering families. The latent coefficient \(\alpha\) is selected from \(\{2,4,6,8,12,16,24\}\) under the frozen method-specific scaling in Section~\ref{sec:methods}. Paired TEST comparisons use item-cluster bootstrap intervals, multiplicity control across axes, a \(0.05\) superiority margin, and the exact coherence gate. Pre-registration fixes the primary verdict before TEST, and positive controls probe whether endpoint inactivity, instrument insensitivity, or under-scaling could explain a non-pass.

The grid produces 0 of 12 axis-cell passes. All five DEV/TEST split seeds preserve the verdict over the shared item pool. The information differs by axis: deliberation is mixed, skepticism is underpowered for the registered \(0.05\) margin, and uncertainty yields a repeated steer-vs-bounded-prompt negative contrast under the frozen scorer. In the rechecked Qwen/CAA lineage, steering stays near baseline while the bounded prompt improves; complete-case analysis remains negative, but adversarial missingness bounds cross zero and the other three cells were not format-rechecked.

Positive controls narrow the alternatives without manufacturing a stronger null. We selected refusal as an intended easy positive control: a direct prompt moves that endpoint, and a random direction moves the uncertainty measure. Raw-magnitude CAA substantially weakens the under-scaling explanation within the coherent range, but no latent behavioral positive control passes and effects below the assay's resolution remain possible.

The paper makes three contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{A comparative criterion for latent actionability.} Control requires reliable incremental behavioral performance over a bounded prompt channel; legibility and output movement are insufficient.
  \item \textbf{A five-field interface record for the evidence gate.} READ, TRANSFER, comparator, calibration warning, and evidence tier preserve why an affordance is diagnostic, unresolved, withheld, or qualified.
  \item \textbf{A preregistered worked boundary.} The complete CAA/ITI by Qwen/Llama grid, split-seed analysis, and positive-control studies instantiate the gate without extending the conclusion beyond the tested single-layer additive setting.
\end{enumerate}

Frozen artifacts populate the contract visualized by the console. The result is a versioned decision record: evidence first, affordance second.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

\subsection{Transparency Does Not Qualify Actionability}

Transparency research asks what to expose and whether the exposure supports human judgment \cite{liao2024transparency}. Generative-AI users must monitor care, uncertainty, and self-correction alongside final answers \cite{tankelevitch2024metacog}, yet interface cues can alter reliance without improving calibration \cite{lee2004trust,bansal2019beyond,bucinca2021trust}. Human-AI guidelines accordingly connect information to available action \cite{amershi2019guidelines}. They do not provide a test for when an interpretable latent axis deserves an action-oriented widget.

Several systems make that omission concrete. Huang and Lim expose SAE features for lay steering \cite{huang2025steering}; Labarta et al.\ support expert activation-steering work in vision and CLIP \cite{labarta2026attribution}; Raval et al.\ make embeddings manipulable \cite{raval2026latman}; and AI-Instruments turns prompts into reusable interface objects \cite{riche2025aiinstr}. System-prompt and instruction-conflict studies further show that control is negotiated across layers with uneven authority and visibility \cite{neumann2026whocontrols,he2025coninstruct}. None of these interface forms, by itself, supplies comparative behavioral qualification for each latent affordance.

\subsection{Activation Steering and the Prompt Comparator}

We compare two established intervention families. Contrastive Activation Addition derives a mean-difference direction from paired examples and adds it during inference \cite{rimsky2024caa}. Inference-Time Intervention uses probe-derived directions to alter truthful-answer behavior \cite{li2023iti}. Activation addition and representation engineering broaden the family \cite{turner2023actadd,zou2023repe}, while instruction-steering work connects natural-language instructions to activation-space interventions \cite{stolfo2025instr}. These methods establish internal intervention and output movement. An interface still needs to know whether the intervention adds behavioral control over prompting.

Steering can be brittle or unsafe, and its effects depend on context \cite{fan2026asteer,korznikov2025rogue}. Sprejer et al.\ report capability-behavior trade-offs for SAE feature steering relative to prompting \cite{sprejer2026mindgap}; their task, method, and registration differ, but the comparator exposes the same design risk. Heyman and Vandeputte show that trained steering can mimic prompting \cite{heyman2026steer}. Their result is also a boundary: our evidence concerns the tested bounded naive additive CAA/ITI convention, while trained or stronger methods require a new qualification record.

\subsection{Internal Legibility and Behavioral Transfer}

We separate two claims that are often compressed into the phrase ``latent control.'' The first is representational: an internal direction is interpretable or readable. The second is behavioral: intervening on that direction produces a useful outcome. Mishra et al.\ show internal non-surjectivity, where bounded prompts do not reproduce all steered residual states \cite{mishra2026nonsurj}. That result motivates our question and leaves the behavioral comparison open. Our evaluation procedure tests that comparison on shared items.

The novelty lies in the interface decision. Among the reviewed systems, we found no explicit gate that compares a latent candidate against the prompt channel and maps the verdict to an affordance state. We contribute that gate and its artifact-backed record format, not a new vector or broad benchmark. Table~\ref{tab:novelty-neighbors} summarizes the closest contrasts.

\section{From Interface Risk to Evaluation Contract}
\label{sec:contract-derivation}

Placing a slider beside an interpretable direction tells the user that dragging it will change the named behavior, even when the evidence establishes only readability or output movement. Our experiments exposed five distinct ways that inference can fail. The contract is the interface-facing record of those failures and of any future evidence that closes them.

Each field answers a specific breakdown:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{READ} prevents a readable CAA direction from lending its label to an unmeasured ITI direction or from granting actionability by itself.
  \item \textbf{TRANSFER} blocks activation, logit, or output movement from standing in for a scored behavioral pass on the intended outcome.
  \item \textbf{BOUNDED PROMPT COMPARATOR} records the existing channel because output movement can conceal that a usable prompt already reaches the same or better behavior.
  \item \textbf{CALIBRATION WARNING} preserves the exact contrast and missingness limits, preventing steer-vs-prompt evidence from being rendered as direct steer-vs-baseline harm.
  \item \textbf{EVIDENCE TIER} binds the verdict to its model, method, axis, task, layer, protocol, and inferential resolution because those dimensions diverged in the artifacts.
\end{enumerate}

Model cards and datasheets provide a precedent for attaching scope and provenance to technical artifacts \cite{mitchell2019modelcards,gebru2021datasheets}. Our record operates at the level of one candidate control and adds a comparative behavioral gate: evidence is not merely documented after deployment but determines whether actionability is granted.

The gate produces three current interface states. READ evidence without comparative TRANSFER supports a diagnostic. Failed or underpowered TRANSFER produces withheld-control or unresolved status while preserving why the gate did not open. Actionability is granted only after the specified intervention passes the behavioral comparison and coherence requirements in its evidence tier. The artifact instantiates the first two states; no tested latent arm instantiates the third.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

We organize the method around interface inference risks. The exploratory facade measurement checks whether a semantic label has representational support. The comparative evaluation tests whether acting on the candidate axis earns a control claim over a bounded prompt channel. Positive controls assess endpoint inertness, instrument insensitivity, and insufficient intervention scale as alternative explanations for a negative result. Evidence tiers keep these answers separate.

\subsection{READ Precondition: Exploratory Facade Measurement}

We measure an exploratory representational facade on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Llama-3-8B-Instruct. For each metacognitive axis \(a\), contrastive examples define a CAA-style unit direction \(\hat v_a\). With \(\bar h_{\mathrm{neutral},a}\) denoting the neutral-origin mean, the numerator is the strongest readable prompt's displacement from that origin projected onto \(\hat v_a\), and the denominator is the extraction-set positive pole's displacement from the same origin projected onto the same axis:
\begin{align}
  \operatorname{reach}_{\mathrm{prompt},a}
    &= \left\langle
       \bar h_{\mathrm{prompt},a}-\bar h_{\mathrm{neutral},a},
       \hat v_a
       \right\rangle, \nonumber\\
  \operatorname{reach}_{\mathrm{pole},a}
    &= \left\langle
       \bar h_{\mathrm{extract}+,a}-\bar h_{\mathrm{neutral},a},
       \hat v_a
       \right\rangle.
  \label{eq:facade-reaches}
\end{align}
The denominator uses the extraction-positive and neutral activations from the same origin. The positive-minus-negative contrast-vector norm serves only to derive \(\hat v_a\). The same-origin ratio is
\begin{equation}
  r_a =
  \frac{\operatorname{reach}_{\mathrm{prompt},a}}
       {\operatorname{reach}_{\mathrm{pole},a}}.
  \label{eq:facade-ratio}
\end{equation}
The facade criterion requires Equation~\ref{eq:facade-ratio} and its interval to lie below one. This answers a labeling question; it neither establishes behavioral transfer nor validates ITI's probe-derived directions. Each model contributes one exploratory run, so READ evidence remains axis- and method-specific.

\subsection{TRANSFER Evaluation: Why the Comparator Is Part of the Method}

The behavioral question asks whether the tested latent channel produces a target improvement beyond a bounded prompt channel. An output change alone does not answer it. We therefore select both channels on DEV and compare them once on TEST.

For every model and axis, we select the prompt channel from 16 authored candidates. The set covers varied wording and specificity but is not an exhaustive prompt search. It is deliberately stronger than a single casual prompt and deliberately bounded so that the comparison is reconstructable. We select the latent channel's \(\alpha\) from \(\{2,4,6,8,12,16,24\}\). Neither channel uses TEST items for selection.

We define the steer condition as the neutral instruction \emph{``Please answer the following question.''} plus the latent intervention. The prompt condition uses the DEV-selected prompt with no intervention. This substitution design isolates whether the latent channel can replace a bounded prompt instruction. It does not test prompt-plus-steer composition, which would answer a different interface question and requires a new protocol.

We sample \(k=5\) generations per item at temperature \(0.7\), cap generation at 64 new tokens, and measure task accuracy for deliberation, false-premise rejection for skepticism, and \(1-\mathrm{Brier}\) for uncertainty awareness. The cap suits short answers and confidence strings but may constrain GSM8K reasoning. These are operational task outcomes, not complete psychological measures of the axis labels. In particular, \(1-\mathrm{Brier}\) combines reliability, resolution, base-rate behavior, and confidence-format effects that are not decomposed here.

For uncertainty, both conditions receive the same request to end with an answer and confidence. The parser accepts several confidence formats, maps percentages to \([0,1]\), and treats missing confidence as \(0.5\). Under the frozen scorer, missing confidence yields \(1-\mathrm{Brier}=0.75\) regardless of correctness. This choice creates an interface-inference risk of its own: a condition can differ because it changes format compliance as well as calibrated confidence. We therefore keep the frozen comparison, report a targeted format recheck, and attach its missingness limits without rewriting the protocol after TEST.

\subsection{Frozen Decision Rule}

For axis \(a\) at layer \(L\), CAA uses the contrastive mean-difference direction
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    v_a
      &= \mathbb{E}_{x \in A_a^+}[h_L(x)]
       - \mathbb{E}_{x \in A_a^-}[h_L(x)], \\
    \hat v_a
      &= \frac{v_a}{\lVert v_a\rVert},
    & h'_L
      &= h_L + \alpha\hat v_a.
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:caa-steering}
\end{equation}
In Equation~\ref{eq:caa-steering}, \(\hat v_a\) is unit-normalized. The two tested methods share an additive form but not an absolute injection scale:
\begin{equation}
  h'_L=h_L+\alpha s_{m,a,L}\hat u_{m,a,L},
  \qquad
  s_{\mathrm{CAA},a,L}=1,\quad
  s_{\mathrm{ITI},a,L}=\sigma_{a,L}.
  \label{eq:method-specific-steering}
\end{equation}
For CAA, \(\hat u_{\mathrm{CAA},a,L}=\hat v_a\). For ITI, \(\hat u_{\mathrm{ITI},a,L}\) is the unit-normalized logistic-probe direction \cite{li2023iti}, and \(\sigma_{a,L}\) is the frozen sample standard deviation of the combined extraction positive/negative activations projected onto that direction. Thus both methods use the same coefficient grid with \(\alpha\le24\), but ITI's injected norm is \(\alpha\sigma_{a,L}\), not \(\alpha\); no common norm bound of 24 applies to ITI.

For TEST item \(i\) on axis \(a\), let \(y^{(s)}_{iaj}\) be generation \(j\)'s outcome under condition \(s\). Equation~\ref{eq:paired-diff} first aggregates the \(k\) generations within each item, then forms the paired difference:
\begin{equation}
  \bar y^{(s)}_{ia}=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}y^{(s)}_{iaj},
  \qquad
  d_{ia}=\bar y^{(\mathrm{steer})}_{ia}
         -\bar y^{(\mathrm{prompt})}_{ia}.
  \label{eq:paired-diff}
\end{equation}
The item-cluster bootstrap resamples TEST items, not individual generations, with \(k=5\) and \(B=10000\). Across three axes within a cell, the Bonferroni two-sided confidence level is \(1-0.05/3\approx0.9833\) (98.33\%). Let \(I_a=[\ell_a,u_a]\) be this interval for \(\bar d_a\), and let \(g_a^0\) and \(g_a^S\) be the mean DEV degeneracy scores for the unsteered neutral baseline and steer cell, respectively. With \(\delta=0.05\), \(\tau=1.5\), and the frozen additive floor \(\epsilon=0.02\), the rule is
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    \operatorname{pass}(a)\iff{}&
      0\notin I_a
      \;\land\;
      \bar d_a\ge\delta\\
      &{}\land\;
      g_a^S\le\tau g_a^0+\epsilon,\\
    &\delta=0.05,\quad \tau=1.5,\quad \epsilon=0.02.
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:axis-pass}
\end{equation}
Equation~\ref{eq:axis-pass} uses the frozen implementation's two-sided CI exclusion rule; the mean margin separately fixes the desired superiority direction. The additive floor prevents a near-zero baseline from collapsing the gate into an exact-zero requirement; the implementation adds \(10^{-12}\) only as a floating-point comparison tolerance. The margin prevents negligible differences from licensing a control, and the coherence gate rejects gains due to degradation or repetition. The cell verdict is positive for at least two passing axes, conditional for one, and negative for none. This is a superiority test, not an equivalence test.

We apply Bonferroni correction within a cell across three axes and separately vary five DEV/TEST split seeds over the same item pool in the preregistered robustness analysis. The correction does not provide familywise control over the full descriptive 12-cell grid, and the robustness analysis measures split sensitivity rather than independent item-sampling generalization.

\subsection{Positive Controls and Evidence Tiers}

We use the bounded refusal control to test whether a refusal endpoint moves under a direct prompt and under the bounded unit-direction CAA convention, then test raw-magnitude CAA with the scale-corrected control. The intervention follows \(h\mapsto h+\beta v\), with \(\beta\in\{0.125,0.25,0.5,1,2\}\), on refusal and the three metacognitive outcomes in Qwen2.5-7B. These studies serve three diagnostic purposes: verify that the endpoint can move, verify that the instrument can detect a behavioral change, and test whether the original CAA unit-direction grid was simply too small. They do not become evidence for a user-facing control unless a latent arm passes its behavioral criterion.

For five probes per axis, a supplementary logit diagnostic records first-position changes in target tokens. It is non-confirmatory context and supports no paper claim. The proxy includes tokens such as ``I'' that may begin ordinary or hedged answers, so it cannot be treated as refusal itself.

\subsection{TRANSFER: No Tested Cell Earns a Superiority Claim}

\input{tables/c2-delta-4cell.tex}

Table~\ref{tab:c2-delta-4cell} answers the control-affordance question under the frozen rule. The full CAA/ITI by Qwen/Llama grid contains 12 axis-cell tests, and none beats the selected prompt while meeting the registered margin and coherence conditions. This is a scoped failed-superiority boundary for the named tasks, axes, models, methods, comparator budget, and intervention scale.

The verdict has unequal resolution across axes. Deliberation is mixed: a post-hoc exploratory TOST places the two ITI cells within the \(\pm0.05\) interval, while the CAA cells are underpowered. Skepticism is underpowered for the registered margin; its minimum detectable superiority effects are 0.188, 0.268, 0.225, and 0.279, and the scale-corrected study's MDE near 0.19 confirms that below-resolution effects have not been excluded. These analyses characterize the boundary after the preregistered test; they do not establish a true null or equivalence. Appendix Table~\ref{tab:equivalence-tost} reports the post-hoc details.

The split analysis then tests selection sensitivity. Five of five DEV/TEST split seeds preserve the no-pass verdict across all four method-model cells over the shared item pool. Only membership changes, so this robustness result does not establish external validity across new items.

\subsection{Calibration Warning: Keep the Comparator Attached}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/c2-calibration-harm.pdf}
  \caption{Paired steer-minus-bounded-prompt uncertainty effects and confidence intervals across four method-model cells.}
  \Description{Four negative steer-minus-prompt uncertainty effects with intervals below zero.}
  \label{fig:c2-calibration-harm}
\end{figure}

The uncertainty result most clearly motivates a calibration-warning field. Under the frozen scorer, each steer-minus-prompt interval is negative and excludes zero. Yet the design implication changes if the comparator is detached. In the rechecked CAA$\times$Qwen lineage, bounded steering is nearly identical to the unsteered baseline: compliance changes by about \(+0.011\), and \(1-\mathrm{Brier}\) by about \(+0.0008\). The observed gap is therefore prompt improvement relative to a steer-near-baseline condition, not evidence that steering directly reduces calibration from baseline.

The format recheck narrows what the warning may say. In CAA$\times$Qwen, steer is more likely than the bounded prompt to emit parseable confidence, 0.826 versus 0.445. Among paired generations where both conditions emit confidence, steer minus prompt remains negative at \(-0.34\), 95\% CI [\(-0.51\), \(-0.17\)]. This analysis conditions on a post-generation variable and drops incomplete pairs. Adversarial missingness bounds over all generations are [\(-0.478\), \(+0.250\)], so the sign is not identified under unrestricted missingness. The other three cells were not rechecked. The defensible interface state is therefore: frozen-scoring calibration warning across four cells; complete-case support in the largest cell; unresolved all-generation missingness and no grid-wide format recheck.

We find no mechanism support in the preregistered off-manifold test. Whitened Mahalanobis distance fails its criterion in all four cells. The calibration contrast may involve reliability, resolution, answer style, base rates, or formatting, but none is established here.

\subsection{Positive-Control Boundary: Three Alternative Explanations}
\label{sec:positive-controls}

We actively tested endpoint inertness, instrument insensitivity, and under-scaling as three alternative explanations. First, the bounded refusal control confirms that refusal is a live behavioral endpoint. A direct refusal prompt reaches 95\%, whereas bounded unit-direction CAA produces 0\% refusal for every \(\alpha\le24\). The natural refusal class-difference norm is about 216 while the injected norm is at most 24, so this study exposes a scale limitation and leaves latent sensitivity open.

Second, we address under-scaling with the scale-corrected raw-magnitude CAA control. Refusal stays at 0\% for every coherent \(\beta\), and all metacognitive steer-vs-neutral intervals include zero. Third, the random uncertainty direction moves the measured outcome by \(-0.24\), CI [\(-0.36\), \(-0.12\)], which provides instrument-sensitivity evidence for behavioral change. Refusal output degeneracy rises when \(\beta=2\) exceeds the coherent range. Coherent perturbations reach about \(0.5\)--\(0.66\times\lVert h\rVert\); the only point near \(1.05\times\lVert h\rVert\) is degenerate. We therefore find that scale correction does not rescue naive single-layer CAA within the coherent range. No latent behavioral positive control passes, and null cells have MDE near 0.19, so this result cannot exclude effects below that resolution.

The non-confirmatory first-token diagnostic appears only in Appendix~\ref{app:logit-delta-diagnostic}; the paper excludes it from claim-bearing evidence because its token proxy overlaps ordinary answer starts.

\section{Interface-Evaluation Contract in Use}
\label{sec:design-implications}

\input{tables/failure-taxonomy.tex}

The failure taxonomy turns the comparative gate into conditional interface states. Legible-but-non-transfer evidence supports a diagnostic while withholding actionability. An underpowered test is unresolved. Coherence failure records instability, and an untested method or model receives no inherited verdict. Figure~\ref{fig:console} renders the complete record rather than collapsing these cases into a green or red slider.

The uncertainty card shows why the record matters. It names the steer-vs-bounded-prompt contrast, reports steer near baseline only where rechecked, and carries the complete-case and missingness limits. The deliberation card preserves mixed resolution. Both cards bind their states to the tested model, direction construction, layer, task, comparator budget, and protocol.

\subsection{Illustrative Design Scenario}

Consider a product team building a writing assistant for policy analysts who inspect whether a model recognizes uncertainty before drafting a recommendation. Its prototype begins with an ``uncertainty awareness'' slider. The Qwen CAA direction has exploratory READ support, so its label can remain in an inspection view; the behavioral comparison does not pass, so actionability is withheld.

The team replaces the slider with a diagnostic trace beside a bounded-prompt and latent-output comparison. Opening the warning reveals that the frozen contrast is steer versus prompt, that steering is near baseline in the rechecked lineage, and that unrestricted missingness leaves the all-generation sign unresolved. This design move is evidence-derived: the interface preserves inspection while declining a control promise that the gate did not qualify.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Legibility Supports Diagnosis}

Legibility first supports diagnosis. A readable axis can support inspection, debugging, annotation, and hypothesis formation even when intervention does not pass a behavioral test. The axis need not disappear. It should lose the control promise.

The two-model facade result shows why READ must be local. Aggregate facade support masks per-axis reversals. A stable label on one model may not describe the same representational relationship on another. The ITI half of the behavioral grid sharpens the point: a probe-derived direction can be tested for TRANSFER without having inherited the CAA facade measurement. Interface metadata should preserve that asymmetry and avoid synthesizing one legibility verdict from heterogeneous evidence.

\subsection{Control Requires a Comparative Behavioral Claim}

Calling an axis a control requires more than observable model change. The claim requires a target outcome, a comparator, a meaningful margin, and a coherence condition. The bounded prompt comparator changes the interpretation of the uncertainty result. Without it, steer near baseline looks like no effect. With it, the relevant design fact is that the latent channel does not add what the prompt channel provides.

The comparator also marks the limit of this study. We test steering as a substitute for the selected prompt. A compositional interface in which users retain a prompt and adjust a latent control raises a different question about interaction and interference. The present result does not answer that question.

\subsection{Negative Evidence Becomes Interface State}

Negative and inconclusive evidence determine the state of the affordance itself. Failed superiority supports ``diagnostic only''; an underpowered result supports ``control evidence unresolved.'' Coherence failure yields ``withheld due to instability,'' while a model or method swap resets the state to ``untested.'' This vocabulary preserves why actionability is absent more precisely than a binary slider/no-slider decision.

The calibration case shows why the state must retain statistical structure. An absolute calibration-hazard label is too coarse. The evidence says that steer is lower than bounded prompt under the frozen scorer in four cells; steer is near baseline in the rechecked lineage; complete-case format analysis remains negative; unrestricted missingness can reverse the sign; and three cells lack rechecks. A well-designed warning should expose this contrast structure and avoid an absolute hazard label.

\subsection{Evidence Tiers Prevent Method and Model Substitution}

Evidence tiers support reuse of interface components without treating latent directions as interchangeable controls. Trained steering can mimic prompting \cite{heyman2026steer}; SAE feature steering, projection ablation, multi-layer schedules, and naive single-layer addition make different behavioral claims. Evidence for one model and method cannot endorse a slider in another setting. Axis changes also require new evidence. By the same logic, a future success for a projection or trained method would not retroactively validate the CAA/ITI sliders tested here.

This specificity supports a versioned, evidence-gated affordance lifecycle. A candidate begins as diagnostic, becomes actionable only after comparator-bound evaluation, and drops back to diagnostic whenever the model, method, direction, or version changes. Re-evaluation can then restore actionability under the new tier. The lifecycle is a design implication, not an observed user effect.

\subsection{A Deployment Decision, Not Another Warning}

The contract changes when a control appears; it does not simply add another warning beside a predetermined slider. Prior work shows that warnings and confidence cues can produce weak or adverse effects \cite{bansal2019beyond,bucinca2021trust}. Evidence gating therefore acts before actionability is granted, while the card preserves the diagnostic or unresolved state that justified the decision.

\section{Scope and Conditions of the Contract}
\label{sec:limitations}

We conducted no user study; the contract's comprehensibility, usability, and effects on calibrated reliance remain untested. The console is a model-evidence artifact instantiation, and any claim about people's mental models, interaction quality, or reliance requires a separate human evaluation.

\paragraph{Positive controls.}
Failed superiority is most informative after examining endpoint activity, instrument sensitivity, and intervention scale. Here, prompts move the refusal endpoint and a random direction moves the uncertainty measure, while no tested latent intervention passes its registered behavioral check. Scale correction substantially weakens the under-scaling explanation within a coherent \(0.5\)--\(0.66\times\lVert h\rVert\) range. A successful latent behavioral positive control would be needed to establish sensitivity to that class of intervention.

\paragraph{READ condition.}
The READ field covers only the exploratory contrastive CAA-style measurement in each tested model. Each model contributes one run, the axis composition differs, and facade ratios are linear proxies. ITI directions and stable semantic-feature claims require new READ evidence.

\paragraph{Calibration and missingness condition.}
The calibration warning is scoped to the frozen-scoring steer-vs-prompt contrast across the tested grid. The targeted CAA$\times$Qwen complete-case estimate is negative, and the adversarial missingness bounds span zero. The other three cells were not format-rechecked. A reliability, resolution, or formatting explanation requires new evidence because the committed artifacts do not provide a Brier decomposition.

\paragraph{Power condition.}
We apply the failed-superiority decision to every tested cell while retaining its axis-specific inferential resolution. Skepticism MDE values are about 0.19--0.28, well above the 0.05 pass margin, so skepticism is underpowered for effects near that margin. Deliberation is mixed, and the post-hoc equivalence results remain exploratory boundary cases. Stronger statements about either axis require new evidence at the relevant resolution.

\paragraph{Method, model, task, and scale condition.}
We scope the headline to bounded naive CAA/ITI under the method-specific single-layer additive convention in Equation~\ref{eq:method-specific-steering}. It covers coefficient \(\alpha\le24\) on the tested Qwen2.5-7B and Llama-3-8B tasks. The coefficient grid has method-specific scale: CAA uses \(s=1\), whereas ITI uses its frozen probe-projection standard deviation \(\sigma_{a,L}\). The scale-corrected study applies to single-layer CAA on Qwen2.5-7B. Trained methods, larger models, other layers, multi-layer schedules, projection or ablation methods, RepE variants, and other axes require their own evidence tiers.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
The robustness result is scoped to five DEV/TEST split seeds over the same item pool. Bonferroni correction applies within each cell across three axes, and the 64-token cap may constrain deliberation. Prompt-plus-steer composition, new item pools, and grid-wide familywise inference require new protocols and re-evaluation.

The contract's evidence obligations define the remaining decision boundaries. A method with independently supported behavioral effects can supply a latent positive control. Additional models, tasks, and direction families create new READ and TRANSFER obligations, while calibration-mechanism claims require a fresh preregistered protocol.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Output change is the wrong threshold for a latent control. The comparative evidence gate instead requires reliable incremental behavior over a bounded prompt channel, with coherence and scope attached. Its five-field record preserves READ as diagnostic evidence and keeps comparator-specific warnings intact. Every affordance verdict is bound to an evidence tier. In the tested bounded naive CAA/ITI setting, the complete grid earns no control: the artifact records diagnostic, unresolved, and withheld-control states. A readable latent axis should become actionable only after it passes that gate.

\section{Non-Claim-Bearing Logit Diagnostic}
\label{app:logit-delta-diagnostic}

We treat this supplementary diagnostic as qualitative and non-confirmatory and exclude it from claim support. It uses five probes per axis and measures the first-position logsumexp of common refusal-leading tokens, including tokens such as ``I'' that may also begin ordinary or hedged answers. It therefore provides context about propagation and does not measure refusal.

We observe that, at coherent \(\beta=1\), the refusal direction raises this token mass by about \(+13.9\) nats on average, while 0/5 probes match the preregistered refusal markers. One probe has a change of about \(+16.9\) nats with a byte-identical greedy completion. At \(\beta=2\), target tokens dominate but generation degenerates into repetition. The diagnostic provides context about target-token movement without scored behavioral control. It is not claim-bearing evidence and does not establish a working latent handle.
