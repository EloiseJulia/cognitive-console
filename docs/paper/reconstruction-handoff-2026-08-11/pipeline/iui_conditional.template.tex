\begin{abstract}
% CONDITIONAL ABSTRACT: rewrite after human-study and prompt-plus-steer branch integration.
Research prototypes expose latent steering as direct control, while Golden Gate Claude publicly demonstrated interaction with an internally modified model. These precedents make qualification a concrete interface problem: once a named direction becomes a control affordance, it makes a behavioral promise. Our comparator-bound procedure separates local READ evidence from behavioral TRANSFER evidence, reserves CONTROL for downstream interface permission, and carries comparator, calibration warning, and evidence tier into a five-field record. We apply the procedure using a DEV-selected prompt comparator drawn from a preregistered bounded candidate set. Across the Qwen and Llama model families, two methods (CAA and ITI), and three behavioral axes, 0 of 12 model--method--axis tests qualified under the reported rule; because no latent behavioral positive control passed, this is a scoped procedural classification rather than a validated assay or a general steering verdict. The shared headline conceals different design problems: skepticism needs more resolution, deliberation is mixed, and uncertainty needs comparator-specific interpretation and measurement repair. We then use a post hoc design mapping, separate from the reported qualification rule, to translate those profiles into four affordance states: \emph{Diagnostic-only}, \emph{Unresolved}, \emph{Unstable}, and \emph{Eligible}. Evaluating that mapping with people and under prompt-plus-steer composition remains future work; the present contribution is a specified procedure and worked account of how evidence changes an interface decision.
\end{abstract}
